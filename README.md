# Abstract
Approaches to bio-based or reclaimed materials often pursue homogeneous behaviors toward prescribed performance; however, a load-bearing structure requires differentiated behaviors at different parts at macroscale. This work develops a graded anisotropic biocomposite that can be customized for lightweight building structures. The sawdust porous matrix of the novel particleboard is reinforced by raw bamboo strips, the density (spacing) and directions of which are tailored to the end use of the structure. The orthogonal, x-shaped, and 4-directional microstructures are developed in consistent with the theoretical model of their effective elasticity. A coherent linearity principle is implemented: (1) the ratio between the structural contribution of the soft matrix to that of the reinforcement equals the cost ratio between the two materials, and (2) the directional stiffness linearly varies with the reinforcement density along the direction. The reinforcement filaments wrapped in hot-pressed sawdust form an anisotropic lattice metamaterial, where the soft matrix prevents the filaments from buckling. The filling pattern of the filaments in the representative volume element (RVE) ensures that the long filaments penetrate the consecutive RVEs. Thus, our method can integrate homogenization–continuum representations of composites (at the micro- and mesoscale) with the discrete structural assembly of slender members (at the macroscale). The effective elastic properties of the composite serve as the interface between the material development at microscale and the structural design at macroscale. The heterogeneous particleboard can form the structural beams of 7–10-m span, with one-third of its volume filled with bamboo strips. We created laboratory mockups of the particleboard RVE and conducted mechanical tests and multiscale topology optimization (TO). The proposed formulation of anisotropic linear elasticity is seamlessly integrated into our extended version of the Solid Isotropic Material with Penalization (SIMP) algorithm. The black–gray–white layouts naturally emerge from TO with anisotropic linear elasticity. The results indicate that the mass customization of lightweight building structures with biocomposites is rewarding. The fabrication process directly specifies the heterogeneous material properties in the production line of Industry 4.0; each product is one of a kind tailored to end use.
